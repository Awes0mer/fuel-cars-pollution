A graph that refits its own links every year

Crude oil → gasoline → fuel use → transport CO2, with miles driven and electric-car adoption feeding in. The strength of each link is stored in HydraDB and refit every year, so each year's correction changes the next prediction. Tested walk-forward against a plain vector-store lookup and a "no change" guess, on years not used to design it.

Pre-registered verdict: PASS (all four parts)

Holdout 2005–2024, n = 18 years. Holdout MAE of the U.S. transport-CO2 change: graph 1.77 pp, no change 2.84, k-NN 2.92, frozen graph 2.29. The rule was committed before any run.

Read this before the verdict.

  • Without 2020 and 2021 (n = 16) the graph's MAE is 1.70 pp: it loses to no change (1.53), loses to k-NN (1.63) and loses to its own frozen version (1.23).
  • Every 95% bootstrap interval of the MAE difference includes zero (table below). With n = 18, the ranking is not statistically settled.
  • Year by year, the graph beats no change in 9 of 18 years and k-NN in 10 of 18.
  • Its largest misses: 2008 (6.85 pp), 2021 (3.46 pp), 2011 (3.38 pp). 2008 is a recession it did not see coming from prices and miles alone.
  • 2009 and 2010 are not scored: FHWA marks 2009 vehicle-miles as unavailable, so both changes are missing.

  • The full walk-forward ran through HydraDB: 2154 HTTP queries in 36.37 s. Graph after the run: 6 Variable, 5 Link, 3 Fit, 240 LinkState, 138 Prediction.
  • Parity with the in-memory store: 138 predictions compared, largest difference 1.25e-10 pp (limit 1e-9). Not bit-identical: in a separate test HydraDB returned 34 of 300 floats one unit in the last place away from what was written; the tiny drift compounds over 48 refits.

How it works

Each year, at the moment the year closes, the graph is asked: by how much did U.S. transport CO2 change? It knows that year's crude oil price, gasoline price, vehicle-miles driven and electric share of new car sales. It does not yet know fuel use or CO2.

  1. Predict. HydraDB is queried for the links pointing into "Fuel use" (a graph walk from the target node), their weights are read, and fuel use is predicted. Then the links into "CO2" are read and the predicted fuel use is pushed one more hop to CO2.
  2. Correct. When the real figures arrive, each target's weights are refit by recursive least squares with forgetting factor λ = 0.9 (picked on 1982–2004 only), and written back to HydraDB. A per-year history node is written for every link, and every prediction is stored as a node too.
  3. Repeat for the next year with the corrected weights.

Links are modelled as nodes, not edges: HydraDB v0.4.0 deduplicates edges on (source, target, type), so a link with a changing weight and a history needs its own node. The same code runs on an in-memory store; the two runs must agree (parity).

Weak link: the EV share → fuel use weight is not pinned down. It is learned from 13 years only and swung between -4.92 and 1.21 after 2012. Read it as "unknown", not as a measured effect of EVs.

Not covered

  • Number of car companies over time: No citable, openly licensed time series was found.
  • Cars in use since the first car (pre-1957): The only official long series found (FHWA MV-200, 1900-1995) is not machine-readable here; see licences page.
  • World cars in use and world car production: No openly licensed source with a verifiable licence was found.
  • World transport CO2: Not downloaded in this build; no sector-level open file was added.
  • EV fleet (stock) size: The openly licensed EV file (Ember) gives new registrations, not the fleet in use.
